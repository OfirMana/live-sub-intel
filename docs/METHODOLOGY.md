# Methodology

How the coach room produces its numbers, its recommendation and its explanation. Every number on screen is either computed from the synthetic event stream or a model score; none is an official tracking measurement or a prediction of the real match.

## Synthetic match

- **Events.** All match actions (passes, carries, pressures, tackles, recoveries, shots, cards, goals) are generated by this project's scripted-possession generator. They are not a real fixture or licensed Premier League data.
- **Fixed seed.** The demo uses seed `20261021`. The same seed and generator version always produce the same match, including who scores and who is booked. Restart rewinds the same scenario; it never draws a new match.
- **Scenario.** The default `lively` scenario (`DEMO_SCENARIO=lively`) adds scripted manager substitutions for both teams and keeps one substitution per team for the end. Scripted changes are part of the event stream. They never follow the app's recommendations.
- **Inspection only.** Inspecting or explaining a plan does not execute it and does not change later events.
- **Player profiles.** Squads, starting line-ups and shirt numbers are historical (2015/16). Ratings start from FIFA 16 values and move by at most 6 points using StatsBomb Open Data evidence from before the match date. See [Data sources](submission/DATA_SOURCES.md) for licences and attribution.

## Match statistics

Computed from events strictly before the displayed checkpoint. A goal scored exactly at the checkpoint counts from the next checkpoint. The browser (`web/coach-room/app.js`) and the server (`src/subintel/match_facts.py`) use the same rules; `tests/test_match_facts.py` checks that both produce identical numbers.

| Statistic | Definition |
|---|---|
| xG | Sum of the synthetic xG on shots and goals. Synthetic input, not a trained shot model. |
| Shots | Shots plus goals. |
| Shots on target | Shots marked on target, plus goals. |
| Pass accuracy | Completed passes ÷ attempted passes. Shown as "—" when no pass was attempted. |
| Possession | Share of known control time (below). Shown as "—" when no control time is known. |
| Goals, assists, cards | An assist needs a successful pass from a teammate linked to the goal. A second yellow counts as a red. |

**Possession estimate.** A successful pass, carry, dribble, recovery, interception or tackle gives a team control. Control lasts until the next control event, and at most 30 seconds without one. Shots, cards, goals, period breaks, miscontrols, dispossessions and failed passes end it. Time with no owner is unknown and is left out of the percentage, so the two teams always sum to 100% of *known* time. The share of the match clock that is known is the possession coverage (in the API and the explanation facts).

## Team needs

Each need runs from 0 (low) to 1 (high) for five dimensions: attacking threat, chance creation, ball progression, ball retention and defending. It combines how the team is performing against expectation with how much each dimension matters in the current game state and coach brief. When a dimension matters more than usual (for example, threat when chasing a win), only a deficit raises the need, so a goal still being needed is not cancelled by earlier good play. The green, amber and red colours (below 0.40, from 0.40, from 0.65) are display cues only, not engine thresholds.

## Fatigue

Fatigue is an estimate from match load: minutes played, scaled by the player's stamina rating, and raised (by at most 25%) when they press or carry the ball more than their profile expects. Distance is inferred, not observed. Fatigue is not a medical measure or a sensor reading.

## Recommendation

1. **Search.** For every available one-for-one substitution, the engine searches six supported formations (4-3-3, 4-2-3-1, 4-4-2, 4-1-4-1, 3-5-2, 3-4-3) and every admissible position assignment across the whole XI. Unsupported positions are blocked, and goalkeepers are not changed.
2. **Benefit.** A plan's benefit, in model units, weighs the XI's change in each dimension by the team's needs and the time left. It is compared with the **best plan that uses no substitution**, so a swap must beat simply repositioning the current players.
3. **Freshness.** Fresh legs add a bounded freshness benefit (weight 1.0 by default). Freshness can justify a change only when the tactical loss stays below the lower of 0.10 and 35% of the freshness benefit, so a key player is not removed just because a fresher, weaker player is on the bench.
4. **Threshold.** A substitution is recommended only when its benefit beats the no-substitution plan by at least **0.15**. Otherwise the result is a repositioning-only plan or no change; both are valid outcomes.
5. **Late win policy (`late-win-v1`).** Only when the objective is a win, the team is level or behind, and the minute is between 70 and 94. The time horizon shifts toward the next 30 minutes and the penalty for weaker defending or retention falls by up to 25%. It never forces a substitution.

Benefits and thresholds are **model scores, not probabilities and not xG**.

## Explanations

1. **Facts.** The server builds verified facts for the inspected plan: the decision, rejection reasons, the main gain and trade-off, shape and position changes, estimated fatigue, the game state, the match statistics above (server-computed, never sent by the browser), and for analysts the benefit against the threshold.
2. **Audiences.**
   - **Broadcaster:** one or two short qualitative sentences, with no scores.
   - **Analyst:** three to five sentences. Always includes a match statistic when one exists, and the benefit against the threshold or the rejection reason.
   - **Coach:** two to four sentences. Covers the game state and brief, urgency, estimated fatigue, role or shape changes, and the downside.
   - Each audience always receives its required facts. The decision, rejection reasons and material trade-offs are never dropped for brevity.
3. **Fact selection.** Microsoft Foundry (`gpt-4.1-mini`) receives the facts and returns only the IDs of the optional facts worth telling. The app validates the IDs and renders the sentences from fixed templates. The model never writes the text, so it cannot invent a number or swap a name.
4. **Fallback.** If the model is unavailable, busy, or returns an invalid selection, the app shows the same verified facts in a default selection, labelled as a template, with a safe error code.
5. **Caching.** Model answers are cached for 10 minutes per checkpoint, coach brief, plan, audience and language. The cache key also carries the template, facts and prompt versions and a fingerprint of the match facts.
6. **Languages.** The explanation language is chosen separately from the page language. There are ten choices: English, Hebrew, Arabic, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese and Simplified Chinese.
   - **English and Hebrew** use hand-written templates.
   - **The other eight:** Foundry translates only the English sentence templates and vocabulary the explanation needs, in one bounded call returning strict JSON (`src/subintel/translation.py`).
     - Player names, numbers, plan identity and statuses are never sent for translation. The server inserts them afterwards through placeholders.
     - A translation is used only if every placeholder survives exactly once, no digit is added or lost, the IDs match, and a negated English sentence (for example "does not pass", "not a probability") is still negated.
     - Validated translations are cached per language and template version, so later explanations reuse them without a call.
   - **Fallback:** if a translation is unavailable or fails validation, the explanation is shown in verified English, labelled as such, with a safe error code.
   - **Text direction:** in Hebrew and Arabic, names and numbers are wrapped in direction isolates, so a score such as 0–1 is never shown reversed.
   - **What validation does and does not prove:** structural validation does not prove that a translation is fluent or exact. The reviewed examples in `tests/fixtures/translations.json` were written by the developer, not by native-speaker linguists.

## Known limitations

- The scenario is scripted, and its weights and heuristics are not calibrated to predict results.
- One substitution at a time. No goalkeeper changes. The search supports 11-player line-ups only.
- Possession is an estimate from event timing, not provider-measured possession.
- Position suitability, role exposure and physical load are heuristics.
- The recommendation does not consider the opponent's specific weaknesses.

## תקציר בעברית

- **המשחק:** סינתטי לגמרי, עם seed קבוע 20261021, כך שאותו משחק חוזר תמיד. החילופים בתסריט אינם תלויים בהמלצות. בדיקת תוכנית אינה מבצעת אותה.
- **הנתונים:** מחושבים רק מאירועים שלפני נקודת הבדיקה, באותם כללים בדפדפן ובשרת.
  - ההחזקה היא אומדן של זמן שליטה. שליטה פגה אחרי 30 שניות בלי אירוע שליטה, וזמן לא ידוע אינו נספר.
  - דיוק מסירות או החזקה שאין להם נתונים מוצגים כ"—".
- **ההמלצה:**
  - חיפוש על כל החילופים, שישה מערכים וכל ההצבות.
  - ההשוואה היא מול התוכנית הטובה ביותר ללא חילוף.
  - הסף הוא 0.15 ביחידות מודל, לא הסתברות.
  - רענון מצדיק חילוף רק כשהירידה הטקטית קטנה.
- **ההסבר:**
  - שלושה קהלים: שדרן, אנליסט ומאמן.
  - עשר שפות להסבר: אנגלית ועברית כתובות ידנית; בשמונה האחרות Foundry מתרגם רק את התבניות, והשרת משבץ את השמות והמספרים. תרגום שלא עבר אימות מוחלף באנגלית עם תווית.
  - המודל ב־Foundry בוחר רק מזהי עובדות מאומתות, והאפליקציה כותבת את המשפטים מתבניות קבועות.
  - כשל מוצג כתבנית מסומנת.
